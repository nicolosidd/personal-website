Daniele Nicolosi is a Product Designer based in Rome, Italy

AI-Powered Reminder App —Life Assistant

Overview

Life Assistant is a voice-first reminder app: you speak, the AI organizes your tasks, agrees on when to remind you and suggests the tool or product to get each one done. Behind it, a contextual marketplace lets brands reach users only when a task signals a real need.

  • Role: Product Engineer — research, user flows, interface design, interactive prototype
  • Platform: Mobile (iOS prototype)

The Problem

We express intentions all day long, but most of them get lost before they become action. Reminder apps ask for typing, lists and date pickers at the worst moment, so many things never get written down.

Meanwhile, brands spend heavily on ads targeted by demographics, reaching people too early, too late or not at all. Two sides of the same gap: people declare what they need, and nobody is listening at the right moment.

The Idea

"Speak your mind, I'll organize the rest."

The home screen centers on one oversized yellow microphone: capturing a thought should take less effort than forgetting it. Its states are explicit, from "Tap to start speaking" to pulsing rings and "Listening — tap to stop".

One tap on the microphone and you talk naturally. Each request becomes a task with a category, a priority and a date, and a short conversation defines the right moment to be reminded.

Every task also comes with suggested actions: tools you already use, or brand offers matching what you just asked for. For brands, that means an audience defined by declared intent, not guesswork.

How It Works

  • Capture — Tap and speak. No fields, no date pickers.
  • Organize — The AI extracts tasks, category, priority and due date.
  • Remind — A conversation sets when to be reminded.
  • Act — Suggested actions link each task to what completes it.

The Business Model: Intent-Based Discovery

Brands upload products and services to a dedicated platform. The matching engine reads each task's category, content and timing and pairs it with the most relevant offer — say "I need running shoes before the half marathon" and the suggestion appears in that task.

  • Users get help completing what they already planned.
  • Brands get a hyper-targeted audience with little wasted reach.
  • The product gets revenue that keeps the core experience free.

If a suggestion feels like an ad, the design has failed.

What's Next & Learnings

The prototype validates the core loop. Next: usability tests on voice interpretation, notification design, a brand dashboard, and metrics such as task completion, suggestion tap-through and opt-out rate.

The biggest lesson: in an AI product the hardest problem isn't the interface, it's the balance — less work for users, a fair way in for brands, and no trust spent along the way.
